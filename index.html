Quantum dots: size sets the color

A quantum dot confines an electron and a hole in a tiny sphere. The smaller the sphere, the higher the confined energies, the wider the band gap, and the bluer the emitted light. Drag the slider to change the radius of a CdSe dot, and switch between measured data and the Brus equation to compare them.

The models

Experimental: the CdSe sizing curve from Yu et al., Chem. Mater. 15, 2854 (2003), a polynomial fitted to measured spectra that gives diameter D from the first absorption peak wavelength λ. The page inverts it to get λ from D = 2R, then Eg = 1239.84/λ. It is fitted for roughly 2 to 8 nm diameters (R = 1 to 4 nm). Emission sits close to this band edge, with a small Stokes shift to slightly longer wavelengths that is not included.

Brus theory: Eg(R) = Eg,bulk + (ħ²π²/2R²)(1/m*e + 1/m*h) − 1.8e²/(4πε₀εR) with Eg,bulk = 1.74 eV, m*e = 0.13 mₑ, m*h = 0.45 mₑ, ε = 10.6 (Brus, J. Phys. Chem. 90, 2555, 1986). It treats the dot as an infinite well, so it overestimates the gap for small dots. Below R ≈ 1.5 nm it predicts ultraviolet light for CdSe, which real dots do not do.

Wavefunctions: lowest s-states of a particle in a spherical well, ψn(r) ∝ sin(nπr/R)/r, with energies ∝ n²/R². The level spacing is schematic. Norris & Bawendi, Phys. Rev. B 53, 16338 (1996), is the standard experimental comparison for CdSe. The wavelength-to-RGB color conversion approximates perception.
